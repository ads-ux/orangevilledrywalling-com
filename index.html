Most drywall work in Orangeville is one of three jobs. The first is a basement apartment: the Town's 2025 rules let a fully serviced lot carry up to three units, and a lot of owners are turning the basement into one, which makes the drywall the fire separation the building inspector signs off rather than a finish. The second is finishing space in the newer subdivisions around the edges of town, where the builder left the basement bare and the framing is still settling, so the finishing has to be planned around nail pops and corner cracks instead of being surprised by them. The third is repair: water marks from an ice-damming winter, stress cracks over doorways, holes from a move, and the textured ceilings that came with a lot of the older homes closer to Broadway. Outside town, in Mono, Amaranth, Grand Valley and Shelburne, the permits run through Dufferin County instead of the Town, and many rural homes are on a septic system, which changes what a basement apartment needs before it is boarded. Every job is quoted after a site visit, because the number of sheets is the least important part of the price.

Local Knowledge

Drywall in Orangeville: Permits, Basement Apartments and the Townships

When an Orangeville drywall job needs a building permit. The Town's building permits page is short on exceptions: you need a permit before you begin new construction or a renovation, and its building and construction page names renovations, demolitions and accessory apartments specifically. Replacing damaged board on walls that stay where they are, patching, and skimming a ceiling are finish work. Finishing a bare basement, moving or adding a wall, re-insulating, adding plumbing or creating an apartment is a permit application first, and the drywall goes up only after the framing, insulation and vapour barrier have been inspected. Applications go to building@orangeville.ca as a PDF or in person at Town Hall, 87 Broadway, and the Building Division is reached at 519-941-0440 ext. 2228. If you are not sure, ask the Town before the board goes up, not after.

What the Town's 2025 rules mean for a basement apartment. Orangeville's Guide to Additional Residential Units allows up to three units on a fully serviced lot: the house plus up to two additional units inside it, or one inside and one in a detached building. Each additional unit is limited to two bedrooms, needs a clear path at least 1.2 metres wide from the street or lane to its entrance, and needs its own parking space of at least 2.6 by 5.5 metres. A building permit is required, and the unit has to meet the Building Code, the Fire Code and conservation authority rules. For the drywall that means the ceiling and the walls between the units are a fire separation built to the approved drawings: the ontario.ca guide to adding a second unit sets it at 30 minutes, or 15 minutes where the smoke alarms in both units are interconnected, and that separation has to be continuous, with no gaps at bulkheads, ducts or pot lights. Check the zoning and parking with the Planning Division (planning@orangeville.ca) before paying for drawings.

Mono, Shelburne, Grand Valley and the rural townships go through the County. Outside the Town of Orangeville, permits for Amaranth, East Garafraxa, Grand Valley, Melancthon, Mono, Mulmur and Shelburne are issued by Dufferin County Building Services through its online portal, usually after the local municipality has given its approval. Many rural houses are on a septic system, and the County publishes a separate basement apartment checklist for that case, because a second unit on a septic system raises questions a town lot does not. In Shelburne specifically, the County has posted a temporary pause on permits for new accessory units, including basement apartments, that need wastewater capacity; anyone planning one there should call the Town of Shelburne first. Erin and Caledon Village are outside Dufferin altogether and are checked against their own town's rules.

Textured ceilings, old compounds and asbestos. Sprayed and trowelled ceiling textures, joint compounds and patching plasters applied from roughly the 1950s to the late 1980s may contain asbestos, and plenty of Orangeville homes from those decades still have the original popcorn ceiling. It cannot be identified by looking at it. Before a popcorn ceiling is scraped or a wall from that era is cut into, a sample goes to a lab, and nothing is disturbed until the result is back. A clean result means scrape and skim or overlay with new board; anything else means a licensed abatement contractor under Ontario's asbestos regulation, O. Reg. 278/05, before we start.

Water, winter and the getting rid of old board. Orangeville sits high, and the long winters bring ice damming at the eaves and water marks on upstairs ceilings in the spring, along with basement seepage after the melt. Drywall that has been soaked through does not recover: it is cut out above the wet line, the cavity is dried and checked, and new board goes in only after the source has been fixed. On disposal, Dufferin County does not run its own waste facility; the GFL transfer station on County Road 11 is privately owned, so check what it takes and charges before loading a trailer. On our jobs the offcuts and tear-out are bagged and removed, not left at the curb.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to finish my basement in Orangeville?

Yes. The Town requires a building permit before a renovation begins, and finishing a basement involves framing, insulation and often plumbing. Patching or replacing damaged board on existing walls is finish work and does not, but if you are unsure, ask the Building Division at 519-941-0440 ext. 2228 before the board goes up.

Can I put an apartment in my basement in Orangeville?

On a fully serviced lot with a single, semi, linked house or townhouse, the Town's 2025 rules allow it. The unit is limited to two bedrooms, needs a 1.2 metre clear path to its entrance and its own parking space, and needs a building permit. The drywall between the units is a fire separation built to the approved drawings.

What does the drywall in a basement apartment have to do?

It is part of the fire separation between the units. Ontario's guide sets it at 30 minutes, or 15 minutes where the smoke alarms in both units are interconnected, and it has to be continuous, including around ducts, bulkheads and pot lights. The board type and layers come from the approved drawings, and the inspector checks it.

I live in Mono or Shelburne. Is it the same process?

The Building Code is the same, but the permit comes from Dufferin County Building Services rather than the Town of Orangeville, usually after your local municipality approves it. In Shelburne there is currently a pause on permits for new basement apartments that need wastewater capacity, so call the Town of Shelburne first.

Is my popcorn ceiling safe to scrape?

Only once a lab test says so. Textured ceilings applied from the 1950s to the late 1980s may contain asbestos and you cannot tell by looking. A sample is tested first; a clean result means scrape and skim or overlay, anything else means licensed abatement before any drywall work.

How much does drywall cost in Orangeville?

The cost guide on this site has 2026 planning ranges by job, from small repairs to full basements. The real number comes in writing after a site visit, because bulkheads, ceiling heights, the finish level and what needs fixing behind the wall move the price more than the square footage.

Do you work outside Orangeville?

Yes. Mono, Amaranth, Shelburne, Grand Valley, Erin and Caledon Village are all covered. Rural properties are quoted the same way, after a site visit, with the permit route checked against the right municipality first.
